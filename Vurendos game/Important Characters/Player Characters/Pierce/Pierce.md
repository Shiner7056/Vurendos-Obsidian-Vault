Following the conclusion of the conflict, Coronot entered the service of a newly appointed nobleman, **Lord Kaleb**, becoming one of his personal guards.

Available records concerning Lord Kaleb's administration describe his rule within Ravenford as peaceful and stable.

No significant allegations of misconduct against Coronot have been identified from this period of his service.

On the contrary, the subject accumulated multiple commendations during his time serving Ravenford and Lord Kaleb.

**VI. VERACITY OF TESTIMONY**

The Court determined that the subject's statements could not reasonably be dismissed as an attempt to escape responsibility.

A **Ward of Truth** had been placed upon Coronot during the relevant proceedings.

Under the conditions imposed by the ward, deliberate falsehood was considered impossible.

Consequently, the Court accepted the fundamental accuracy of the subject's testimony.

This did not absolve him of responsibility for his actions.

Rather, it established that the subject's account of possession, loss of control, and subsequent realisation was genuine.

The Court therefore distinguished between:

1. The acts committed by Pierce Coronot;
    
2. The subject's conscious intention to commit those acts;
    
3. The supernatural circumstances surrounding the incident; and
    
4. The subject's conduct following the termination of the possession.

**VII. FINDINGS OF THE COURT**

After reviewing the testimony, available evidence, military records, witness accounts, and magical verification, the jury found **Pierce Coronot guilty**.

The verdict was not issued on the basis that the subject had fabricated his possession.

Nor was it determined that the subject had knowingly planned the deaths of those under his command.

Rather, the Court determined that Coronot remained the individual responsible for the acts committed under his name.

The jury nevertheless reached a second conclusion:

**Pierce Coronot was not beyond redemption.**

The Court therefore declined to impose the maximum available punishment.

**VIII. SENTENCE AND DISPOSITION**

Pierce Coronot was permitted to retain his life.

He was further permitted to continue carrying weapons.

**IX. ASSESSMENT OF THE SUBJECT**

The Court records make no determination that Pierce Coronot should be considered innocent.

He was found guilty.

Nor do the records support the conclusion that he acted with ordinary malicious intent during the events in question.

The evidence instead presents an unusual case involving supernatural influence, loss of personal control, and subsequent acceptance of responsibility.

Of particular note is the subject's willingness to provide a complete account of his actions despite the knowledge that doing so would likely result in conviction.

His statements regarding the deaths of his own companions were not accompanied by attempts to shift blame entirely onto them or onto the unidentified witch.

The subject repeatedly accepted responsibility for what had occurred.

This was considered during sentencing.

The Court's conclusion was therefore that Coronot had committed acts deserving of punishment, while simultaneously demonstrating sufficient remorse and capacity for redemption that execution was deemed unnecessary.
