#Kalyth #Player

Kalyth Gareth Virette was born two years after the Great Plague War, into the royal line of Astelrath. From her earliest years, she was kept carefully sheltered from the outside world, always accompanied by one of the kingdom’s royal guards. For a long time, Kalyth believed this was simply how life was meant to be. When visitors came to the palace, she was kept out of sight, confined to her rooms and away from curious eyes.

Slavery remained a deeply entrenched institution within Astelrath, and the revelation that the Queen had borne a tiefling child so early in her rule would have been politically disastrous. Nixie’s decision to hide her daughter was not born of shame or disdain, but of fear and overprotection. She wished to spare Kalyth from prejudice at an age when she could not yet defend herself, while also avoiding the appearance of weakness in the eyes of nobles and rival nations during the fragile period of abolition.

Only a select few knew of Kalyth’s existence: both of her mothers, the royal guards, and a handful of the Queen’s most trusted confidants, most of whom have since faded from Kalyth’s memory. During her years of isolation, the guard assigned to her became increasingly protective, ensuring she lacked for nothing and often indulging her small comforts. Though he was formally her godfather, Kalyth came to see Aldwin as a third parent. She admired him not only as a protector, but as someone whose loyalty was unwavering. Aldwin never questioned her worth or existence; to him, she was simply a child who deserved safety, and he took it upon himself to ensure that no harm ever came to her.

Around this time, Kalyth gained a brother, Valrus. He appeared far more human than tiefling, bearing the Queen’s pale complexion and lacking horns or a tail, marked only by yellow eyes and pointed ears reminiscent of Veltish. From an early age, Valrus displayed arrogance and pride, fueled largely by the rapid development of his magical abilities through his draconic bloodline. Away from the watchful presence of their parents, he often looked down on others.

Valrus devoted himself to understanding the political machinery of Astelrath, while Kalyth pursued swordplay under Aldwin’s guidance and deepened her devotion to Famis. Nixie openly praised Kalyth’s compassion and discipline, a habit that quietly irritated her daughter and only deepened the unspoken tension between the siblings.

When Kalyth was around ten years old, she was finally introduced to the wider world and allowed to roam freely throughout the palace. Yet even with this newfound freedom, she often chose seclusion unless duty required otherwise. Though she was no longer hidden, Kalyth remained reserved, shaped by an early understanding that the deepest care was often the quietest. Nixie carried immense guilt for the years of isolation and made earnest attempts to make amends, but Kalyth did not resent her for it. She found comfort in stillness and genuinely enjoyed the quiet life she had known.

Her tiefling mother, Veltish, was a consistent presence in her life, but rarely a stationary one. Always on the move and often away from the palace, Veltish’s absences meant that Kalyth never came to know her as deeply as she might have wished.

When the war with Kartus began, Nixie sent her children away from the capital so that trusted advisers could see to their safety. She sent them to different locations, each with armed escorts. Kalyth chose Dorizus, a place already familiar to her, while Valrus was sent to Fort Teelrath.

The majority of the available royal garrison was assigned elsewhere, leaving fewer resources to spare for Kalyth’s journey. As a result, Nixie turned to outside support, commissioning protection from the Order of Scales, an elite order known for their discipline and their bonded dragons. As the war escalated, bandit activity along the main roads increased, making even short journeys dangerous.

Kalyth traveled with a small escort: one royal guard and three members of the Order of Scales, all Aarakocra. The guard rode inside the wagon with her, keeping watch as she passed the time reading and engaging him in quiet conversation. Outside, the three members of the Order patrolled on foot, their winged forms moving with practiced awareness through the surrounding terrain.

Midway through the journey, as the wagon passed through a stretch of dense forest, a sharp hissing sound cut through the air.

A heartbeat later, something struck the wagon with a violent bang, jolting it to a halt. The horses screamed. For a brief, suffocating moment, the woods fell silent.

Then the screaming began.

Steel clashed outside the wagon, accompanied by the heavy thrum of beating wings. Shadows flickered across the wagon walls as the Order of Scales engaged, their movements swift and coordinated.

The wagon door flew open as a man in rugged armor lunged inside, a scarf pulled tight over the lower half of his face. The royal guard reacted instantly. The two grappled in the cramped space, the guard driving the bandit back with a brutal kick that sent him tumbling from the cart.

There was no time to breathe.

A sudden rush of wind tore through the doorway as an Aarakocra dropped down beside Kalyth, talons crunching into the dirt. His feathers were darkened with ash and sap, wings flaring briefly as he took in the scene. His sharp gaze flicked from Kalyth to the chaos outside and back again.

“Princess,” the Aarakocra said firmly, already reaching for her. “We’re moving.”

His taloned hand closed around her arm, grip secure but controlled, and he hauled her from the wagon in one decisive motion. Kalyth stumbled as her feet hit the ground, her book slipping from her grasp and disappearing into the undergrowth, but the Aarakocra did not slow. He turned immediately, pulling her away from the road and into the trees.

Behind them, the royal guard and the remaining two members of the Order of Scales formed a defensive line around the wagon. Steel rang out as they met the bandits head-on, wings beating hard as the Order fought to hold the attackers back. A dragon’s roar tore through the forest canopy, followed by a burst of fire as one of the riders took to the air, the flames briefly illuminating the chaos below.

The Aarakocra ran.

He moved with relentless speed, wings partially unfurled to keep his balance as he dragged Kalyth through the underbrush. Branches tore at her cloak and roots threatened to send her sprawling, but he adjusted instinctively, never releasing his grip.

Kalyth risked a glance over her shoulder.

She caught a glimpse of the wagon engulfed in violence—steel flashing, wings beating, the royal guard and the two remaining members of the Order of Scales holding the line as they fought to buy her escape. Then the Aarakocra pulled her forward again, forcing her focus ahead.

They did not stop until the sounds of battle dulled behind them, fading beneath the pounding of her heart and the rasp of her breath. At last, the Aarakocra veered sharply into a narrow ravine hidden by brush and stone, bringing her to a halt only once they were fully concealed.

He released her arm then, stepping slightly in front of her as he scanned the treeline, wings folding tight against his back.

“You’re clear,” he said at last, voice steady despite the chaos they had left behind. “They’ll hold.”

Kalyth nodded, though her hands trembled as she steadied herself. Somewhere behind them, steel still rang and fire still roared but she had been pulled from the heart of the danger.

The Aarakocra hurried Kalyth onward, urging her out of the forest and toward the city beyond. They did not follow the road, instead weaving through uneven terrain and dense undergrowth, choosing distance over speed. By the time night fell, Kalyth’s legs were screaming in protest, each step heavier than the last.

When they finally stopped, Kalyth sank down almost immediately, her back pressing against the base of a tree as she struggled to steady her breathing. Her hands shook faintly as she rested them in her lap, the adrenaline of escape finally giving way to exhaustion.

The Aarakocra did not sit.

He moved in a slow, deliberate circle around their resting place, talons barely disturbing the forest floor as he scanned the darkness. His head tilted at intervals, listening intently, wings shifting just enough to stay loose and ready. The forest around them felt wrong—too quiet, as though it were holding its breath.

Kalyth watched him from where she sat, guilt twisting low in her chest. Others were still fighting. Bleeding. All so she could sit here, too tired to stand.

She opened her mouth to speak, perhaps to thank him, perhaps to apologize—but no words came. The Aarakocra paused briefly, meeting her gaze.

“We’ll rest but not for long.”

The warning lingered in the air long after he moved away again.

Shortly after her sixteenth birthday, Kalyth began accompanying Aldwin and his small troop on missions throughout the kingdom. These assignments dealt with bandits, unrest, and matters requiring discretion beyond the reach of the standard guard. Through them, Kalyth

honed her skills as both a cleric and a swordswoman. More importantly, she was given the chance to see Astelrath beyond palace walls.

The country was imperfect, scarred by inequality, unrest, and lingering prejudice but she found beauty in that imperfection. Helping its people, even in small ways, felt more meaningful than any courtly obligation ever had.

The people of Astelrath rarely recognized Kalyth’s true identity; she was known simply as a quiet cleric who traveled alongside Aldwin’s troop, offering assistance wherever it was needed. She made a habit of leaving before any gratitude could linger. Only a few ever connected her to the royal line. Kalyth found comfort in being known for her actions rather than her blood connection to the crown, and it allowed her to remain largely under the radar of the nobles she so often disliked.

During one of her missions with the troop, Kalyth was allowed to take command for the first time. It was meant to be a test an opportunity for Aldwin to see how much she had learned under his guidance but the responsibility of making every decision left her deeply uneasy. More than anything, she feared someone would be hurt because of her choices.

The mission itself seemed simple: a large group of bandits had been terrorizing a small town near the western border. Though the bandits outnumbered them, Kalyth trusted in the troop’s training and experience. Still, she hesitated to launch an immediate assault on the camp, unwilling to risk lives by rushing in. That hesitation gave the bandits time to notice the approaching soldiers and prepare.

When the fight finally broke out, the bandits put up far more resistance than anticipated. The clash was brief but violent. In the chaos, one of the younger soldiers was dragged to the ground, a bandit raising their weapon for the killing blow. Aldwin stepped in without hesitation, interposing himself between them. The strike shattered his shield and drove deep into his shield arm, biting into the bone of his shoulder.

Aldwin survived, and he never blamed Kalyth for her caution. If anything, he praised her desire to protect her soldiers. That forgiveness weighed heavier on her than any accusation could have. His shoulder never fully healed, and though Aldwin rarely spoke of it, Kalyth noticed the signs, the way he struggled to hold his shield for long during training, the stiffness and pain that returned whenever the cold set in.

Kalyth carried the guilt quietly. She went out of her way to help Aldwin in small, unspoken ways, seeking forgiveness for a failing he never named but one she believed was entirely her own.

Kalyth’s relationship with her brother was strained in ways neither of them wished to openly address. Valrus possessed a sharp mind and a natural attunement to magic, and he made no secret of his ambition to shape the future of Astelrath. To him, Kalyth’s reluctance to appear at court and her preference for anonymity represented a squandered opportunity. Though he rarely spoke harshly to her, his disapproval surfaced in subtler ways offhand remarks about her absence from court, or pointed suggestions that she was avoiding her responsibilities as the Queen’s eldest child.

Kalyth, in turn, found Valrus’s pride and fixation on power deeply unsettling, particularly his belief in his own birthright. Rather than confront him directly, she chose distance, allowing silence to fill the space where understanding never quite took root. Nixie wished desperately for her children to grow closer and made several attempts to bridge the divide between them, but each effort only seemed to widen the gap, leaving both siblings further withdrawn from one another.

Kalyth’s greatest desire is to be more than her bloodline. She longs to build a life shaped by her own choices rather than one decided for her by birth into a royal line. Power and recognition hold little appeal for her; instead, she seeks freedom beyond titles and lineage. Kalyth wishes to be defined by her service, her faith, and the quiet acts of compassion through which she believes true worth is found.

In the years that followed, Kalyth continued to travel across Astelrath rather than remain entrenched in high society like her brother. She preferred the company of common folk to that of visiting nobles, finding their lives and struggles far more grounding. Through these journeys, she experienced countless ways of living and forged quiet connections with the people she served, deepening both her compassion and her resolve.
